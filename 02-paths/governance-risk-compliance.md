# GRC (Governance, Risk & Compliance) - Deep Dive

GRC is the business side of cybersecurity. You do not hack or defend systems directly. Instead, you make sure the organization follows rules, manages risk, and stays compliant. This is the easiest path to enter if you do not want to code.

## 1. What is GRC?

GRC stands for Governance, Risk, and Compliance.

**Governance:** How the organization makes security decisions.
**Risk:** Identifying and managing threats to the business.
**Compliance:** Following laws, regulations, and standards.

**What GRC people do:**

- Write security policies
- Assess risks
- Prepare for audits
- Ensure compliance
- Train employees
- Report to leadership

**Why GRC matters:**

- Companies must follow laws (fines if they don't)
- Risks must be managed (or the business fails)
- Policies prevent chaos
- Auditors need evidence
- Leadership needs reports

**The reality:**

- Lots of writing
- Lots of meetings
- Detail-oriented work
- Less technical than other paths
- Good work-life balance
- High demand for skilled people

**GRC vs Technical Security:**

| Aspect | GRC | Technical |
|--------|-----|-----------|
| Focus | Policy, risk, compliance | Systems, tools |
| Daily | Writing, meetings | Hands-on |
| Skills | Business, communication | Technical |
| Coding | Not needed | Required |
| Entry | Easier | Harder |
| Salary | Good | Higher |

## 2. Every Role Explained in Detail

### GRC Analyst

**What you do:**

You help the organization manage risk and stay compliant.

**Daily tasks:**

- Review policies
- Assess risks
- Track compliance
- Prepare reports
- Support audits
- Answer questionnaires
- Document everything

**Example day:**

```
09:00 - Check emails, prioritize tasks
09:30 - Review new policy draft
10:30 - Risk assessment meeting
12:00 - Lunch
13:00 - Update risk register
14:30 - Answer vendor security questionnaire
16:00 - Prepare compliance report
17:00 - Document findings
```

**Example task — risk assessment:**

```
Task: Assess risk of new cloud vendor

Step 1: Identify assets (what data will they access?)
Step 2: Identify threats (breach, outage, insider)
Step 3: Identify vulnerabilities (their security posture)
Step 4: Rate likelihood (1-5)
Step 5: Rate impact (1-5)
Step 6: Calculate risk score (likelihood x impact)
Step 7: Recommend controls
Step 8: Document in risk register
```

**Skills you need:**

- Risk assessment
- Policy writing
- Frameworks (ISO 27001, NIST)
- Communication
- Attention to detail
- Excel/spreadsheets

**Tools you use:**

- GRC platforms (ServiceNow, Archer)
- Excel
- Risk registers
- Policy templates
- Compliance checklists

**Challenges:**

- Boring tasks
- Convincing people to follow rules
- Keeping up with regulations
- Limited budget
- Proving value

**How to succeed:**

- Learn frameworks deeply
- Build relationships
- Automate what you can
- Communicate clearly
- Stay organized

## 6. Frameworks and Regulations

### ISO 27001

**What it is:** International standard for information security.

**Structure:**
```
1. Scope
2. Normative references
3. Terms
4. Context
5. Leadership
6. Planning
7. Support
8. Operation
9. Performance
10. Improvement
```

**Why it matters:** Global recognition, shows commitment.

### NIST CSF

**What it is:** US framework for cybersecurity.

**Core functions:**
```
1. Identify
2. Protect
3. Detect
4. Respond
5. Recover
```

**Why it matters:** Flexible, widely used.

### SOC 2

**What it is:** Audit for service organizations.

**Trust principles:**
```
1. Security
2. Availability
3. Processing integrity
4. Confidentiality
5. Privacy
```

**Why it matters:** Required by many customers.

### PCI DSS

**What it is:** Standard for payment card data.

**Key requirements:**
```
1. Firewalls
2. No default passwords
3. Protect stored data
4. Encrypt transmission
5. Antivirus
6. Secure systems
7. Access control
8. Unique IDs
9. Physical security
10. Logging
11. Test security
12. Policy
```

**Why it matters:** Required to accept cards.

### GDPR

**What it is:** EU privacy regulation.

**Key principles:**
```
1. Lawfulness
2. Purpose limitation
3. Data minimization
4. Accuracy
5. Storage limitation
6. Integrity
7. Accountability
```

**Why it matters:** Huge fines for violations.

### HIPAA

**What it is:** US health data regulation.

**Key rules:**
```
1. Privacy Rule
2. Security Rule
3. Breach Notification
4. Enforcement
```

**Why it matters:** Required for health data.

## 7. Risk Management Process

### Step 1: Identify risks

```
- Brainstorm with teams
- Review past incidents
- Check threat intel
- Use frameworks
- Document everything
```

### Step 2: Assess risks

```
Likelihood (1-5):
1 = Rare
2 = Unlikely
3 = Possible
4 = Likely
5 = Almost certain

Impact (1-5):
1 = Negligible
2 = Minor
3 = Moderate
4 = Major
5 = Severe

Risk score = Likelihood x Impact
```

### Step 3: Prioritize risks

```
Score 1-5: Low
Score 6-12: Medium
Score 13-20: High
Score 21-25: Critical
```

### Step 4: Treat risks

```
Options:
1. Mitigate (add controls)
2. Transfer (insurance)
3. Avoid (stop activity)
4. Accept (document)
```

### Step 5: Monitor risks

```
- Track in register
- Review regularly
- Update as needed
- Report to leadership
```

### Example risk register

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Score | Treatment |
|------|------------|--------|-------|-----------|
| Ransomware | 4 | 5 | 20 | Mitigate |
| Phishing | 5 | 3 | 15 | Mitigate |
| Insider threat | 2 | 5 | 10 | Monitor |
| Power outage | 2 | 3 | 6 | Transfer |
| Old software | 3 | 4 | 12 | Mitigate |
